01 — INPUT PIPELINE

Multi-Modal MRI Fusion

Each training sample is a single 2D axial slice drawn from a 3D BraTS 2020 volume. Rather than processing a grayscale image, the model receives a 4-channel tensor of shape (128, 128, 4) where each channel carries a distinct MRI modality, stacked depth-wise like an RGB image with an extra fourth channel.

Before stacking, every modality undergoes volume-wise Z-score normalization — the mean and standard deviation of all non-zero (brain-tissue) voxels are computed per 3D volume, and each voxel is rescaled to (v − μ) / (σ + ε). This removes scanner-specific intensity bias while preserving inter-tissue contrast, which is critical for cross-site BraTS data.

02 — CONTRACTING PATH

The Encoder

The encoder is the feature-extraction half of the U. It progressively compresses spatial resolution while expanding filter depth, forcing the network to learn increasingly abstract representations of tumor morphology. Each encoding level consists of two Conv2D layers with relu activation and he_normal kernel initialization, followed by Dropout for regularization and a MaxPooling2D layer that halves spatial dimensions.

He Normal initialization is specifically suited for ReLU activations — it maintains the variance of activations across layers, avoiding vanishing or exploding gradients during training on the relatively small BraTS dataset.

CONV BLOCK STRUCTURE
Per-Level Operation
Each encoder level applies: Conv2D(filters, 3×3, relu, he_normal) → Dropout(0.1) → Conv2D(filters, 3×3, relu, he_normal) → MaxPooling2D(2×2). The same-padding ensures spatial dimensions are only reduced by the pooling step, not the convolutions.
WHY MAXPOOLING
Spatial Compression Strategy
MaxPooling2D(2×2) halves the spatial dimensions at each level (128→64→32→16), creating a 4-level pyramid. This forces the network to build spatially invariant feature representations, while progressively doubling filter count captures richer feature combinations at each scale.
03 — BOTTLENECK

Abstract Feature Compression

The bottleneck sits at the bottom of the U at 16×16 spatial resolution with 512 feature maps — the densest information point in the network. No pooling occurs here. Two consecutive Conv2D(512) layers with Dropout(0.2) process the encoded representation before it begins its upward journey through the decoder.

At this compressed scale, each spatial location has a large receptive field that spans the majority of the 128×128 input. This allows the bottleneck to reason about global context — the overall shape and location of tumor mass — before the decoder reconstructs the fine-grained pixel-level boundary. The higher dropout rate (0.2 vs 0.1 in encoder levels) acts as stronger regularization at the point of maximum abstraction.

🔥
Peak Receptive Field
At 16×16, each neuron in the bottleneck has a theoretical receptive field covering the entire 128×128 input, enabling global reasoning about lesion position, shape, and surrounding anatomy — information the decoder will use to guide precise boundary reconstruction.
04 — SKIP CONNECTIONS

Preserving Spatial Precision

Skip connections are the defining innovation of U-Net and the primary reason it outperforms standard encoder-decoder networks for medical image segmentation. During encoding, MaxPooling progressively discards spatial detail — fine edges, vessel boundaries, and the millimetre-scale tumor margins that radiologists rely on clinically.

Skip connections bypass the bottleneck entirely: the feature maps produced at each encoder level (before pooling) are stored and later concatenated channel-wise with the corresponding decoder level's upsampled output. This gives the decoder direct access to both the high-resolution spatial detail from the encoder and the deep semantic context from the bottleneck simultaneously.

🔗
Concatenation Mechanism
How encoder feature maps are merged into the decoder
At each decoder level, a Conv2DTranspose doubles the spatial resolution of the decoder tensor. The result is then concatenated along the channel axis with the encoder feature map from the matching spatial level. The merged tensor (with double the channels) is then processed by two Conv2D layers that learn to fuse semantic and spatial information.

PROBLEM SOLVED
Gradient Flow
Skip connections create direct gradient pathways from the loss to early encoder layers, mitigating the vanishing gradient problem and enabling effective end-to-end training even with many layers.
MEDICAL RELEVANCE
Boundary Precision
Tumor boundaries in MRI are often only a few pixels wide. Without skip connections, the decoder can only place tumors approximately. Skip connections restore the sub-pixel detail needed for clinical-grade delineation.
INFORMATION THEORY
Multi-Scale Fusion
By merging features at 3 different resolutions (128×128, 64×64, 32×32), the final output integrates texture, shape, and location cues simultaneously — mimicking how a radiologist cross-references multiple scales of detail.
05 — EXPANSIVE PATH

The Decoder

The decoder mirrors the encoder in structure, progressively expanding spatial resolution back to 128×128 while halving filter depth. At each of the three decoder levels, a transposed convolution upsamples the current feature map, a skip connection concatenates the corresponding encoder features, and two standard convolutions refine the merged representation.

Unlike the encoder which uses MaxPooling for downsampling, the decoder uses learned upsampling via Conv2DTranspose. This is preferable to bilinear upsampling because the upsampling weights are trained jointly with the rest of the network, allowing the decoder to learn task-specific spatial reconstruction patterns rather than relying on a fixed interpolation kernel.

DECODER L3
32 × 32 Resolution
Conv2DTranspose(256, 2×2, strides=2) upsamples from 16×16. Concatenated with Encoder L3 skip. Two Conv2D(256) layers refine the merged 512-channel tensor back to 256 channels.
DECODER L2
64 × 64 Resolution
Conv2DTranspose(128, 2×2, strides=2) upsamples from 32×32. Concatenated with Encoder L2 skip. Two Conv2D(128) layers integrate coarse semantics with mid-level spatial structure.
DECODER L1
128 × 128 Resolution
Conv2DTranspose(64, 2×2, strides=2) restores full resolution. Concatenated with Encoder L1 skip — the highest-fidelity spatial features. Final Conv2D(64) layers prepare the feature map for the output head.
06 — OUTPUT & LOSS STRATEGY

Binary Mask & Hybrid Loss

The final layer is a Conv2D(1, kernel_size=1, activation='sigmoid') that projects the 64-channel decoder output into a single-channel probability map of shape (128, 128, 1). Each pixel value represents the model's probability that the pixel belongs to tumor tissue. A threshold of 0.5 converts this into a hard binary mask at inference time.

The model is optimized with a Hybrid Loss combining Binary Cross-Entropy and Dice Loss at equal 0.5 weighting. This combination is standard practice in medical segmentation because the two losses address fundamentally different failure modes of pure single-loss training.

📊
Binary Cross-Entropy
BCE = −(y·log(ŷ)
+ (1−y)·log(1−ŷ))
Operates pixel-by-pixel. Penalises individual pixel misclassifications strongly, ensuring the model learns precise local boundaries. However, in highly imbalanced datasets where tumor pixels are rare, BCE alone can be dominated by the large mass of background pixels, causing the model to predict all-black masks.
🎯
Dice Loss
Dice = 1 − (2·|A∩B| + ε)
          / (|A| + |B| + ε)
Operates on the full prediction mask as a set overlap metric. Directly optimises the Dice coefficient — the standard BraTS evaluation metric — and is naturally robust to class imbalance because it measures fractional overlap rather than pixel-wise accuracy. The smoothing term ε prevents division by zero on empty slices.
⚡
Hybrid Loss
L = 0.5 × BCE
   + 0.5 × (1 − Dice)
The equal-weight combination provides pixel-precise boundary supervision from BCE while Dice Loss prevents class-imbalance collapse and directly maximises the evaluation metric. Together they produce sharper boundaries than Dice alone while maintaining better recall on small tumor regions than BCE alone. Adam at lr=1e-4 provides stable, adaptive convergence.
07 — TRAINING CONFIGURATION

Optimization Setup

The model was trained end-to-end on BraTS 2020 2D axial slices. Only slices containing at least one positive tumor pixel were included in training to reduce the prevalence of purely negative examples and focus learning on informative samples.
